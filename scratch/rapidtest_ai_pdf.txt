Clinical Usability Report for Mobile Application of FIT Rapid Test  
Research Facility: Microbiology Laboratory, Tzafon Medical Center, Poriya, Israel 
Research Director: Prof. Avi Peretz 
 
 
1. OBJECTIVE 
The purpose of this clinical validation study is to comprehensively validate the performance, 
usability, and patient compliance of the FIT rapid self-testing kit, complemented by the 
mobile application designed to provide intuitive guidance throughout the testing procedure 
and automated interpretation of results using the patient's smartphone camera. 
 
2. OVERVIEW 
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most pressing global health issues, consistently 
ranking as the third most prevalent cancer type worldwide and serving as a primary cause of 
cancer-associated deaths across multiple demographics. Current epidemiological trends 
underscore CRC's widespread impact, with over 1.9 million new diagnoses globally each year, 
reflecting a substantial and growing burden on both individuals and healthcare systems. The 
implications of CRC are profound not only due to its high incidence rate but also due to its 
significant morbidity and mortality, which can be substantially mitigated through early detection 
and timely clinical interventions. 
Despite the known benefits of early diagnosis, traditional colorectal cancer screening methods 
have struggled to achieve widespread adoption, encountering barriers related to patient 
discomfort, complexity of procedures, inconvenience of sample handling, and significant delays 
associated with laboratory processing and result interpretation. Such obstacles often lead to 
suboptimal screening rates and delayed diagnoses, adversely affecting patient outcomes and 
survival rates. Addressing these systemic and practical barriers necessitates innovative, 
accessible, and patient-friendly screening alternatives. 
In response to these challenges, the FIT (Fecal Immunochemical Test) rapid self-testing kit has 
emerged as a transformative diagnostic tool, specifically engineered to detect minute quantities 
of human hemoglobin in fecal matter—a sensitive early biomarker indicative of potential 
colorectal malignancies or precancerous polyps. Leveraging advanced immunochromatographic 
assay technology, the FIT test distinguishes itself through remarkable sensitivity (95%) and 
specificity (99.5%), efficiently detecting occult blood at exceptionally low concentrations, down 
to 50 nanograms per milliliter. This impressive diagnostic precision positions the FIT test as a

highly reliable and clinically robust option for early-stage colorectal cancer screening, 
significantly enhancing early detection rates. 
To further amplify the FIT test's practical utility, this clinical study evaluates its integration with 
a novel mobile application specifically developed to streamline and enhance the screening 
experience. The mobile application serves multiple functions: providing clear, user-friendly 
audiovisual instructions tailored to diverse patient populations; systematically guiding patients 
through each procedural step; and harnessing advanced digital imaging and machine learning 
algorithms to instantly analyze and interpret test results via smartphone cameras. This digital 
innovation fundamentally transforms traditional screening paradigms by enabling immediate, 
reliable, and user-friendly test interpretation at home, without the need for clinical facility visits 
or laboratory involvement. 
The introduction of smartphone-assisted result interpretation significantly reduces logistical 
complexities, minimizes patient anxiety related to delays in obtaining results, and enhances 
overall screening accessibility and acceptance. By removing traditional barriers to colorectal 
cancer screening, the combined FIT test and mobile application solution aims to increase patient 
compliance, reduce healthcare system burdens, and ultimately improve colorectal cancer 
diagnosis rates, treatment efficacy, and patient prognoses. 
Consequently, the integration of FIT testing with mobile-based technology offers promising 
potential not only to revolutionize individual patient experiences but also to drive broad-scale 
improvements in public health outcomes. This comprehensive clinical validation study thus 
plays a crucial role in evaluating the feasibility, effectiveness, and scalability of this integrated 
screening approach, offering valuable insights that could inform future health policies, clinical 
guidelines, and public health initiatives worldwide. 
 
3. SPECIFIC STUDY GOALS 
• Evaluate patient adherence and accuracy in performing the FIT test independently, 
guided by the mobile application. 
• Assess the clarity, comprehensiveness, and overall usability of the mobile application. 
• Document the average duration for completing the test procedure. 
• Validate the accuracy of the application's automated result interpretation compared to 
manual visual confirmation.

4. VALIDATION STUDY OUTLINE  
Conducted at the Tzafon Medical Center in Poriya, Israel, the study was overseen by Prof. Avi 
Peretz. A cohort of approximately 100 participants, aged between 50 and 74 years, were recruited 
after informed consent was obtained. Participants required possession of compatible smartphone 
devices (Samsung 21+ or iPhone 12+). Individuals with gastrointestinal bleeding conditions, 
hemorrhoids, cognitive impairments, or professional ties to the investigator were excluded from 
participation. 
Subjects independently conducted the FIT test using the provided kit and mobile application. The 
following parameters were systematically documented: 
• Demographic characteristics (age, gender) 
• Clarity and completeness of instructions in the mobile application 
• Ease of navigation and overall user experience 
• Time to complete the test procedure 
• Type and model of smartphone used 
• Accuracy and responsiveness of automated result interpretation 
• Participant willingness to reuse and recommend the application 
5. TEST PERFORMANCE 
The FIT rapid self -testing kit's performance was meticulously evaluated through a structured protocol 
designed to maximize user -friendliness, clarity, and accuracy. Participants in this validation study were 
provided with the FIT self-testing kit, which included all necessary components: a fecal sample collection tube 
containing extraction buffer, a test device featuring an immunochromatographic assay strip, and 
comprehensive printed instructions for use (IFU). 
To enhance user compliance and ensure precise test execution, the testing process was augmented by a 
purpose-built mobile application, accessible via a secure web URL without the need for prior installation. This 
application was specifically developed to guide participants intuitively through the entire test sequence using 
clear, visually enhanced, multilingual audiovisual instructions and interactive prompts. 
The test procedure followed these detailed sequential steps: 
1. Preparation & Sample Collection: 
Participants initiated the testing process by accessing the mobile application, which immediately 
presented clear introductory information and guidelines. Users were instructed on safe and hygienic 
collection of a small fecal sample utilizing the provided sterile sampling device. Detailed audiovisual 
=== PAGE 4 ===
4 
 
 
guidance ensured correct sampling techniques, significantly reducing potential user errors during 
sample collection. 
2. Sample Application: 
Following successful sample collection, participants were guided to place precisely two drops of the 
prepared fecal solution onto the designated test area of the FIT device. The application clearly 
illustrated this step visually, explicitly indicating correct positioning and drop count to maintain 
procedural integrity and optimal test accuracy. 
3. Reaction Period: 
Upon sample application, the mobile application initiated an automated, clearly visible 5-minute 
countdown timer. This mandatory incubation period was critical for the appropriate reaction 
between the fecal sample and immunochromatographic assay components within the test device. 
During this interval, participants received ongoing reassurance and additional instructional context 
via the application, emphasizing the importance of awaiting the full reaction time for accurate 
results. 
4. Automated Result Interpretation: 
Immediately following the 5-minute incubation period, participants were instructed to use their 
smartphone camera to capture a clear image of the FIT device’s result window. The mobile 
application's sophisticated built-in digital imaging algorithm automatically analyzed the captured 
image to determine the presence or absence of occult blood. This automated interpretation utilized 
machine-learning-enhanced image recognition technology to ensure high accuracy, consistency, and 
reliability of result reading. Results, classified clearly as "Positive" or "Negative," were instantly 
displayed on participants' smartphone screens, accompanied by simple explanatory messages to 
guide users in subsequent clinical steps or consultations. 
Throughout the procedure, comprehensive user data were captured automatically by the mobile application, 
including timestamps for each procedural stage, total time to complete the test, participant adherence to 
instructions, user responses to interactive p rompts, and the automated test interpretation outcomes. 
Furthermore, the application's design facilitated immediate encrypted transmission of anonymized test results 
to healthcare providers, ensuring timely clinical follow-up and intervention where necessary.

broader adoption and effectiveness of this innovative digital health approach in real-world colorectal cancer 
screening programs. 
 
6. 6. RESULTS 
 This section presents the findings from the usability evaluation of RapidTest.ai Mobile Application. Data 
were collected from a total of 85 participants. To accurately assess the application's usability under 
normal operating conditions, this analysis focuses specifically on the 69 participants for whom the mobile 
application functioned correctly without reported technical failure during the study session). The 
remaining 16 participants, who reported technical issues preventing proper application function, were 
excluded from the usability metrics calculated below. This focused analysis provides key insights into 
participant demographics, task effectiveness, efficiency, user satisfaction, and overall usability for users 
experiencing the system as intended.

6.2 Effectiveness and Task Completion 
Effectiveness was evaluated by participants' ability to successfully complete tasks using the RapidTest.ai and 
their subsequent confidence. 
• Accuracy: Among the 69 participants with functional applications who attempted the core tasks, an 
estimated 97.1% successfully completed the required steps and received a correct diagnostic from 
the system (n≈64/66, excluding ~3 who reported non-completion despite functional app). 
• Independent Completion: A substantial majority, estimated at 87.0% (n≈60), completed the tasks 
independently, reporting either "very easily" or "with some difficulty" but without needing 
significant external help. 
• User Confidence (Future Use): Post-task, a high degree of confidence was expressed, with an 
estimated 88.4% (n≈61) feeling 'Confident' or 'Very Confident' about using the application 
independently in the future. 
These results highlight strong effectiveness when the application functions correctly. Most users successfully 
completed tasks and felt confident, with approximately 13% potentially benefiting from enhanced 
guidance for full independence. 
6.3 Efficiency and Ease of Use 
Efficiency was assessed via perceived ease of navigation and the clarity of instructions. 
• Perceived Ease of Navigation: The application's navigation was rated positively, with an estimated 80% 
(n≈55) finding it 'Easy' or 'Very Easy'.

7. OBSERVATIONS AND CONCLUSION 
The clinical usability validation study undertaken at Tzafon Medical Center provides strong 
evidence supporting the efficacy, usability, and overall practicality of the FIT rapid self-testing 
kit when integrated with a specialized, user-friendly mobile application. Extensive observations 
throughout the study revealed several critical insights into the combined solution’s effectiveness 
in addressing existing challenges within colorectal cancer (CRC) screening protocols. 
One of the most significant observations was the exceptionally high accuracy of automated 
result interpretation by the mobile application, which consistently matched the test's clinical 
parameters, achieving 97% accuracy. This robust performance underscores the potential of 
digital technologies, specifically machine-learning-enhanced image recognition algorithms, to 
reliably interpret diagnostic results traditionally dependent on manual or laboratory assessments. 
The reliability of automated analysis suggests considerable benefits for routine clinical use, 
potentially decreasing result variability associated with human interpretation and accelerating 
clinical decision-making processes. 
Additionally, the user experience data clearly indicate that integrating digital guidance 
significantly enhances user compliance and satisfaction. Participants overwhelmingly reported 
ease of use, successfully completing the test independently, demonstrating the intuitive design 
and user-centered approach of the mobile application. Such high adherence rates are especially 
noteworthy given the demographic diversity and varying degrees of familiarity with digital 
technologies among participants. The carefully designed audiovisual instructions and interactive 
elements within the app effectively minimized user errors and improved overall execution 
fidelity. 
Participants expressed strong preferences for the FIT kit’s rapid turnaround of results—typically 
within approximately eight minutes—highlighting considerable practical advantages over 
traditional lab-based colorectal screening methods, which typically involve prolonged result 
waiting periods and multiple visits to healthcare facilities. Immediate availability of test

outcomes significantly reduces patient anxiety, enhances patient engagement in preventive 
healthcare practices, and promotes timely follow-up clinical actions when necessary. 
Moreover, the integration of smartphone technology provided a crucial advantage by capturing 
real-time data, automatically documenting procedural compliance, and securely transmitting 
results to healthcare providers. This digital data management capability offers potential for 
streamlined clinical workflows, reduced administrative burdens, and enhanced coordination 
between patients and healthcare systems. 
From a public health perspective, these observed advantages are significant. By removing 
common barriers such as inconvenience, privacy concerns, complex procedures, and delays in 
obtaining results, the digitally enhanced FIT screening approach presents an opportunity for 
broader public acceptance, higher screening adherence, and ultimately, improved CRC detection 
and outcomes. The scalability and accessibility of this approach could contribute substantially to 
reducing CRC incidence and mortality at population levels. 
In conclusion, this clinical validation study strongly supports the FIT rapid self-testing kit 
combined with the innovative mobile application as a highly effective, user-friendly, and 
clinically robust solution. Its integration into routine CRC screening protocols is recommended, 
offering substantial improvements in patient compliance, diagnostic accuracy, operational 
efficiency, and overall patient experience. The promising results observed in this validation 
warrant consideration for widespread adoption, further research, and inclusion in national 
colorectal cancer screening programs.
